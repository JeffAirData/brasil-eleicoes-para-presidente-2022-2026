Flávio Bolsonaro led the first round of Brazil's 2026 presidential election. Lula won the runoff, 51.8% to 48.2%.

I compared the official open data from the TSE, Brazil's electoral court, in three ways.

Who is who: Lula (Workers' Party, PT, left) is the sitting president. Flávio Bolsonaro (Liberal Party, PL, right) is a senator and the son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, whom Lula beat in 2022.

1) Runoff 2022 vs runoff 2026
- Lula got 50.9% of the valid votes against Jair Bolsonaro in 2022 and 51.8% against Flávio in 2026.
- He carried 13 of the 27 states both times. Two states switched: Amapá went to Lula, Tocantins to the Bolsonaros.
- Lula's share rose in 16 of 27 states. The biggest gain was in the Federal District (Brasília): 4.5 points.
- Voters abroad gave Lula 53.9% (51.3% in 2022).

2) First round vs runoff, 2026
- First round: Flávio 47.0%, Lula 45.2%. Runoff: Lula 51.8%.
- Only one state changed sides between the rounds: Minas Gerais, from Flávio to Lula.
- Lula gained 10.4 million votes between the rounds; Flávio gained 3.7 million. The eliminated candidates had 9.3 million votes; the rest came from higher turnout (3.0 million) and fewer blank and spoiled ballots (1.8 million). These are net balances: they show where the increase came from, not who voted for whom.
- Lula's margin over Flávio improved in all 27 states and abroad, including the states he lost.

3) Goiás, city by city
- Lula went from 31 to 40 of every 100 votes in the state. Flávio went from 54 to 60 and won Goiás.
- Lula led in 28 of the 246 cities in the first round and in 41 in the runoff. 15 cities changed their preferred candidate.
- Ronaldo Caiado (PSD), the state's former governor, had 12 of every 100 votes in the first round. Where he was strongest, Flávio tended to grow more in the runoff.
